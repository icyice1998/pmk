Technical report · Protocol RF 08_1_2563, version 2

Prediction of Carbapenem-Resistant Culture Results from Routine Hospital Records Using Long Short-Term Memory Networks

Project: Prediction of drug-resistant pathogens using a neural network machine learning model through routine hospital electronic health record

Project period: from approval by the research review subcommittee to 30 June 2027 (data collection to 30 June 2026; data preparation 1 to 31 July 2026; model refinement and testing 1 August 2026 to 30 June 2027)

Cohorts: CRPA, CRAB, CRE and CRO · Last updated 21 November 2026

Cohorts

Outcome definition and index day

  • The index day (day 0) of a positive episode is the date of the patient's first carbapenem-resistant culture. The index day of a negative episode is a randomly selected culture date of a patient who never had a carbapenem-resistant culture.
  • Patients with a carbapenem-resistant culture before the index day were excluded from the cohort.
  • Screening specimens (stool, rectal swab, perianal and rectal samples) were excluded from outcome labelling.
  • CRPA: P. aeruginosa resistant, intermediate or dose-dependent to meropenem, imipenem or doripenem. CRE: Enterobacterales resistant, intermediate or dose-dependent to ertapenem, imipenem, meropenem or doripenem, with imipenem results disregarded for Proteus, Morganella and Providencia. CRAB: A. baumannii resistant, intermediate or dose-dependent to meropenem, imipenem or doripenem. CRO: a culture meeting the CRPA, CRAB or CRE definition.

Inclusion and exclusion criteria

Inclusion criteria

  1. At least one clinical (non-screening) culture of the target organism group with a carbapenem susceptibility interpretation recorded as resistant, intermediate, dose-dependent or susceptible. CRPA: P. aeruginosa tested against meropenem, imipenem or doripenem. CRAB: A. baumannii tested against the same three agents. CRE: Enterobacterales tested against ertapenem, imipenem, meropenem or doripenem. CRO: any of the three groups.
  2. Resistant episode: the patient's first culture resistant, intermediate or dose-dependent to at least one tested carbapenem; its date is the index day.
  3. Non-resistant episode: a patient with at least one tested culture and no carbapenem-resistant result at any time; one tested-culture date, chosen at random with a fixed seed, is the index day.
  4. Index day on or after 1 January 2012 and not later than the last culture date in the database.
  5. One episode per patient. No restriction on admission status or length of stay.

Exclusion criteria

  1. Screening specimens (stool, rectum, perianal, rectal swab, rectal) were not used as outcome cultures.
  2. Imipenem results of Proteus mirabilis, Morganella morganii and Providencia species were disregarded in the CRE definition.
  3. Patients with a carbapenem-resistant result before the index day were excluded. For CRE this included the laboratory CRE flag on clinical cultures and CRE-positive rectal screening; for CRO, a prior result in any of the three groups.

Methods

Predictors

Each episode was represented as a sequence of 91 daily time steps, from day −90 to day 0. Each time step contained binary indicators for administration of each intravenous antibiotic by name, administration of each antibiotic class, admitting department, ward type (general, intensive care, isolation) and inter-ward transfer. Age (standardised using training-set statistics) and sex were supplied as constant channels at every time step. Only intravenous antibiotics were included. The number of channels differs slightly between cohorts (Table 1).

Data partitioning

Patients were assigned to the development or test partition by a deterministic hash of the pseudonymised hospital number, so that all episodes of a patient fall in one partition. Twenty per cent of development patients, selected at random with a fixed seed and grouped by patient, formed the validation set. Overlap of patients between training, validation and test sets was checked programmatically before every training run.

Model and training

The network comprised a masking layer, three stacked LSTM layers of 100 units, dropout (rate 0.2) and batch normalisation after each of the first two LSTM layers, and a single sigmoid output unit. Models were trained with the Adam optimiser and binary cross-entropy loss, a batch size of 64 and a maximum of 300 epochs. Training stopped when validation area under the precision-recall curve (AUPRC) failed to improve by at least 0.0005 for five consecutive epochs, and the weights of the best epoch were restored. Five models were trained per cohort using random seeds 42 to 46.

Model selection and evaluation

The final model for each cohort was the seed with the highest validation AUPRC; the test set was not used for selection. Performance was evaluated once on the test set. Confidence intervals were obtained from 1,000 bootstrap resamples of test patients. Calibration slope and intercept were estimated by logistic regression of the outcome on the log-odds of the predicted probability. Threshold-based metrics used the cut-off that maximised the Youden index on the validation set.

Model explanation

Feature attributions were computed with the gradient-based SHAP explainer (shap 0.43.0) for the final model of each cohort, using 200 training patients as the background distribution and 1,000 test patients as the explained sample. Attributions are expressed in units of predicted probability. For each patient, attributions were summed over the 91 days; the mean absolute value across patients is reported as mean |SHAP|. The direction of association of an antibiotic is the sign of its mean total SHAP value among explained patients who received it at least once in the window.

Limitations

  • The study is retrospective and uses data from a single hospital system. No temporal or external validation was performed, so performance in other settings or periods is unknown.
  • Validation data were used both for early stopping and for selecting the final seed. Test-set estimates are unaffected by this choice, but the validation metrics are optimistic.
  • The 91-day window includes the index day. Antibiotics administered on the index day may reflect empirical therapy for a suspected resistant infection rather than an antecedent risk factor.
  • SHAP values describe how the trained models use their inputs. They do not establish a causal effect of any antibiotic on resistance, and correlated antibiotics share attribution.
  • Prevalence of resistance differs substantially between cohorts (Table 1), which affects precision-recall metrics and predictive values; these should be compared across cohorts with this in mind.
